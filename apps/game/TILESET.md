# Tileset

The tiles the game draws, generated from the tile registry (`packages/core/src/local/tiles.ts`)
by `npm run tileset:doc`. Do not edit this file by hand: change the registry and run the script.

## Format

- **Tile size.** A tile is 1.5 m of ground (one cell of the floor plans): about a person's
  stride and a half. Art is **16 × 16 pixels**, drawn **top-down** (see
  `docs/design/navigation.md`, *How the local map is drawn*). The game shows it at 2× by default
  (+ and - zoom from 1× to 4×).
- **Layers.** Each position has a **ground** tile (fills the whole square); sometimes an **object**
  on top of it (wall, door, tree, bed), drawn with a transparent background; and, on open ground
  with no object, sometimes a **decoration** (a tuft of grass, pebbles), drawn only and placed by
  position (see *Decorations* below).
- **Files.** Put the sheets and their index in `apps/game/public/tileset/`:
  - one or more sheets (PNG), any number of 16 × 16 cells in a grid;
  - `tileset.json`, the index:

```json
{
  "tileSize": 16,
  "image": "tileset.png",
  "sheets": { "nature": "nature.png", "water": "water.png" },
  "tiles": {
    "grass": [0, 0],
    "grass.1": [1, 0],
    "wall_stone#5": [5, 3],
    "tree_broadleaf": { "sheet": "nature", "at": [2, 0], "size": [1, 2] },
    "boulder": { "sheet": "nature", "rect": [0, 64, 18, 18], "offset": [-1, -2] },
    "water_shallow#15": { "sheet": "water", "at": [0, 0], "frames": 4, "ms": 180 },
    "campfire": { "at": [[0, 5], [1, 5], [2, 5]], "ms": 120 },
    "pebbles": { "sheet": "nature", "at": [6, 6] }
  },
  "standIns": {
    "grass_lush": "grass",
    "needle_floor": { "as": "forest_floor", "tint": "#e8dcc4" }
  }
}
```

- **A frame** is given as `[column, row]` on the default sheet (`image`), or as an object:
  - `"sheet"`: a sheet named in `sheets` (the default sheet if left out);
  - `"at": [column, row]` with `"size": [w, h]` in cells (1 × 1 if left out), or `"rect": [x, y, w, h]`
    in pixels;
  - `"offset": [dx, dy]`: where the picture's top-left corner sits from the tile's, in pixels. If
    left out, a picture larger than a tile **stands on the tile**: centred across it, its foot on
    the tile's foot, rising above it. Keep this to a few pixels (a person's head, the top of a
    barrel): the map is top-down;
  - **animation:** `"frames": n` (laid out to the right, or along `"step": [dx, dy]` in cells) or
    `"at"` as a list of cells; each shown `"ms"` milliseconds (200 if left out).
- **Frame names.** The tile's name (`grass`); numbered variants after a dot (`grass.1`,
  `grass.2`), picked at random by position; and join frames after a hash (`wall_stone#5`).
- **Seasons and snow.** Any frame may also be drawn for a season or for snow lying, after an at
  sign, before any variant or join: `grass@autumn`, `tree_broadleaf@winter.1`,
  `leaves_birch@autumn#5`, `grass@snow`. Seasons are `spring`, `summer`, `autumn` and
  `winter`; `snow` is used out of doors whenever snow lies, before the season's frame. A frame
  drawn for a season is used as it is; without one, the plain frame is tinted for the season as
  before (grass yellowing, broadleaf trees turning). Where snow lies on ground with no `@snow`
  frame, a white cover is laid over it; once it lies deep (15 cm or more), the `snow` tile itself
  is drawn instead, if the tileset has one. Edges made from a tile's art use its season's art too.
- **Joins (mask4).** Tiles marked *join* connect to neighbours of the same group (all walls,
  doors and windows are one group; water is another). Their frame number is the sum of the
  sides that join: **north 1, east 2, south 4, west 8** (0 to 15). A join tile drawn only as its
  plain name is used for every side.
- **Stand-ins.** A tile with no art of its own can borrow another's (every variant and join of it),
  multiplied by a `tint` if one is given, until it has art: `"grass_lush": "grass"`.
- **Anything missing** is drawn as a placeholder (its colour and glyph below), so art can be
  added a few tiles at a time and the game always runs. Mistakes in `tileset.json` are listed in
  the developer console and the rest is used.

Suggested sheet layout: one row per section below, in this order, with the 16 join frames of a
tile in a row of their own; animations along a row.

## Ground edges

Where two kinds of ground meet on one level, the terrain later in this list spreads a ragged edge over the one before (grass over earth, sand over water). Tiles in no terrain (paving, planks, floors) keep straight edges; so does a change of level, which has its own line.

Edges are drawn on a grid offset by half a tile: each **edge frame** is a 16 × 16 picture centred on the corner where four tiles meet, showing the terrain over the corners that are of it (or of a terrain later in the list) and the ground beneath showing through elsewhere (transparent). Its number is the sum of those corners: **north-west 1, north-east 2, south-east 4, south-west 8** (1 to 14; 0 and 15 are never drawn). Name them `edge_<terrain>#<number>`, with variants after a dot (`edge_grass.1#3`).

**Drawing them is optional:** a terrain without edge frames gets them made from each tile's own art, its pattern carried on over the corner and cut to a ragged line with a darker rim, so any tileset blends from the start.

|---|---|---|---|
| `edge_deep` | 14 | `water_deep`, `river_deep` | Deep water reaching into the shallows. |
| `edge_shallow` | 14 | `water_shallow`, `river` | Shallow water lapping over deep. |
| `edge_mud` | 14 | `mud`, `marsh`, `ditch` | Wet mud and puddles at the water's edge. |
| `edge_sand` | 14 | `sand`, `desert_sand`, `salt` | Sand spreading over the shallows, with a wet rim. |
| `edge_earth` | 14 | `dirt`, `dry_earth`, `road`, `track`, `path`, `yard`, `field`, `garden_bed`, `vineyard`, `grave_ground`, `spoil` | Bare earth with a crumbling edge. |

## Trees across levels

Trees stand across levels (see `docs/design/navigation.md`): on the ground a tree is its **trunk**, cut across as seen from above, with **roots** on the tiles beside it; on the levels above (seen with < and >) its trunk again, then **branches** and **leaves**. Each kind has these frames (transparent background):

- `trunk_<kind>`: the trunk on one tile;
- `trunk2_<kind>#<quarter>`: a trunk two tiles across (old broadleaf and jungle trees), in four quarters: north-west 1, north-east 2, south-east 4, south-west 8, the trunk's middle being the corner they share;
- `roots_<kind>#<side>`: roots reaching into a tile from the side toward the trunk (north 1, east 2, south 4, west 8);
- `branch_<kind>#<joins>` and `leaves_<kind>#<joins>`: 16 frames each, joining as walls do (north 1, east 2, south 4, west 8); a branch joins branches and the trunk, leaves join leaves.

## Figures

People (and later creatures) moving about the map, drawn by the way they face: **north, east, south, west** (`n`, `e`, `s`, `w`). Seen from above and a little in front, as the furniture is. For each facing:

- `<figure>_<facing>`: standing (an animation if wished, such as breathing);
- `<figure>_walk_<facing>`: a walk cycle, as an animation (`frames` and `ms`: a step from one tile to the next takes about 130 ms to show).

A figure may be taller than a tile (16 × 24, say): it stands on its tile, its head rising into the one above. A figure drawn facing east only is turned about for west. Without art, the game draws a placeholder person in the wanderer's own skin and hair.

| Figure | Frames | Draw |
|---|---|---|
| `person` | `person_n`, `person_e`, `person_s`, `person_w`, and `person_walk_` of each | A person seen from above and a little in front (head, shoulders, arms, legs): the wanderer, and later everyone else. |

## Roofs

Every building has a roof (its shape and material from its climate and use, as the atlas's town pictures have them), drawn over it from straight above as an option: shown unless the wanderer is inside (faded over any of the inside seen through a door or window), always, or never (Shift+R). Each tile of a roof lies on one **plane** of it: a slope facing north, east, south or west (`n`, `e`, `s`, `w`), the ridge running east and west or north and south (`ridge_ew`, `ridge_ns`), a flat roof (`flat`), or the peak of a cone or dome (`peak`). Frames are named `roof_<material>_<plane>`, with joins as walls have them (`roof_tile_s#5`; the open sides are the eaves), and seasons and snow as any frame (`roof_thatch_n@snow`). One picture without a number serves for every join. The light falls from the north: draw slopes facing north lighter and those facing south darker.
